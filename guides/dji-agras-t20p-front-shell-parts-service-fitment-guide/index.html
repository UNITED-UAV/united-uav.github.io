DJI Agras T20P Front Shell Parts: An Evidence-Based Service and Fitment Guide

Published 2026-09-14 by UNITED UAV Official

A front-shell issue on an agricultural UAV is not merely a question of whether a plastic cover looks presentable. The shell surrounds interfaces that need to remain accessible and protected, and a collision or chemical-exposure event can affect fasteners, cable clearance, camera or sensing views, and the integrity of adjacent assemblies. This guide uses the ten material-numbered variants in the UNITED UAV DJI Agras T20P front-shell listing as a controlled identification vocabulary. It explains how an operator can document a suspected defect, communicate the exact part requirement to qualified service personnel, and evaluate the returned aircraft without treating a catalog entry as a repair procedure or a guarantee of compatibility. DJI's own T20P safety and maintenance instructions take precedence; damaged-aircraft repair and replacement should be handled through DJI Support or an authorized DJI dealer.

1. Define the job before choosing a part

Start with an event description, not a shopping-cart selection. Record whether the concern followed a collision, transport compression, routine washing, chemical exposure, vibration, or an unexplained change in fit. These origins lead to different inspection boundaries. An impact that appears to have cracked only the upper cover may also have loaded a clip, a lower mating edge, a mounting point, or a nearby connector. A wash-related problem can involve a cover or port that was already unseated. The initial question is therefore whether the aircraft is fit to remain out of service pending qualified inspection, not which visible plastic piece is cheapest to replace. Do not fly a suspected damaged aircraft to reproduce a symptom.

The T20P is a spraying and spreading aircraft, so the front area exists in a system exposed to field contaminants and repeated cleaning. DJI describes the aircraft as having radar and binocular-vision functions as well as an FPV camera, but that does not mean every front-shell part directly protects every sensor. Treat the relationship between a particular crack and any sensor fault as a hypothesis until a technician traces the installed configuration. Conversely, do not dismiss a cosmetic-looking opening when it is close to a cable pass-through, latch, or port cover. Define the defect location, affected function, and observable evidence separately. This prevents a shell-only purchase from being mistaken for a completed airworthiness assessment.

2. Treat the ten listed material numbers as distinct identities

The official UNITED UAV store listing identifies ten selectable entries. Item 1 is YC.JG.MY000776, Spraying Adaptive Cable Sleeve. Item 2 is YC.JG.ZS002432, Front Lower Shell 2. Item 3 is FL.SC.BB000001, Acetate Fabric. Item 4 is YC.JG.ZS002283, Front Lower Shell. Item 5 is YC.JG.ZS002282, Front Upper Shell. Item 6 is YC.WJ.L00870, Screw M30-HC060060-55-85. Item 7 is YC.JG.ZS002422, Outer Shell Buckle. Item 8 is YC.ST.LL000235, T30-HC00800080-057030-5103-N. Item 9 is YC.JG.ZS000998, Rear Shell Small Buckle Base. Item 10 is YC.ST.LL000236, T20-PC00400040-040010-5103-N. These are cataloged choices, not a claim that one order includes all ten parts.

For purchasing and service records, preserve four separate fields: displayed item number, exact material number, material name, and the selected store variant or SKU. A variant's internal SKU may carry an added suffix, so do not silently replace a material number with a truncated or suffixed SKU. In particular, Front Lower Shell 2 and Front Lower Shell have different material numbers; the numeral is not proof that one supersedes the other. Likewise, the Rear Shell Small Buckle Base appears within the front-shell product listing, but the grouping alone is not proof of its mounting location on every aircraft configuration. The dealer should resolve physical fit from the aircraft serial number, applicable parts documentation, revision, and the inspected component.

3. Build a non-invasive damage map

Before service handoff, collect a context photograph of the entire aircraft and then close views of the front, left, right, underside, and the affected interface. Include a scale reference without obscuring the defect. Photograph the damage before cleaning if contamination patterns may matter, then record any cleaning performed under the applicable DJI guidance. Note gaps, asymmetry, stress whitening, broken retaining features, abrasion, deformation, trapped debris, evidence of liquid ingress, and any contact between a shell edge and an adjacent cable. These are observations, not a diagnosis of internal damage. If a part is loose, avoid pressing it back into place just to improve a photograph; that can erase the evidence of a failed retention feature.

Give each observation an identifier such as F01 for a front-upper crack, F02 for a lower seam gap, and F03 for a nearby cable sleeve that appears displaced. On a simple sketch, mark where each image was taken and the direction of view. Separate what is visible from what is merely reported by the operator. For example: 'F02: daylight visible along the left lower seam in an unloaded, powered-off aircraft' is stronger than 'lower shell is broken.' The latter makes a part-level conclusion before examining the latch and mating surface. A reproducible description lets a service technician decide whether the suspected shell, buckle, fastener, or another assembly requires inspection.

4. Distinguish deformation, fracture and retention failure

A crack ending at a fastener boss suggests a different inspection path from a broad scuff on an outer panel. A missing clip can make two undamaged shells appear misaligned. A displaced cable sleeve can leave the exterior nearly normal while compromising the intended route. Delamination or lifted fabric-like material raises a different materials question from an actual shell fracture. Record the type of indication and its position relative to seams, latches, ports and cable openings; do not assign a load rating, cause, or continued-flight allowance from a photograph. A single visual symptom can have several causes, and a single impact can create more than one symptom.

Use a three-level triage language in the handoff. 'Observed' means directly visible or documented. 'Suspected' means a plausible interpretation requiring inspection. 'Not established' means the team lacks evidence. A photographed loose buckle may be observed; whether the buckle is the sole cause of a seam gap is suspected; whether the internal mount is sound is not established. That distinction matters when a replacement order is placed in advance of a physical inspection. It also makes the final service report auditable: the technician can close each original observation and record newly found defects instead of merely reporting that a new cover was fitted.

5. Give the cable sleeve its own inspection question

The store describes YC.JG.MY000776 as a protective sleeve associated with the spraying adapter cable route in the front-shell area. That is enough to justify a specific question to the service provider: is the sleeve present, intact, seated, and appropriate for the cable and shell revision on this aircraft? It is not enough to infer the cable's electrical rating, connector pinout, bend radius, sealing method, or a field-replacement technique. If wear marks or a pinch point are visible, document their location without pulling the cable through the opening. A sleeve order without checking the cable beneath it may leave the actual defect unresolved.

The service request should distinguish a sleeve defect from evidence of cable insulation damage, connector contamination, or a harness that has moved relative to its original path. Ask the qualified technician to evaluate the complete affected route, including clearances in the assembled state, and to document whether the cable itself needs replacement. Do not perform a powered function check to investigate a suspected damaged cable before the technician has made the electrical and mechanical assessment. A successful spray-system self-check later in the acceptance process does not retroactively prove that a visually compromised cable route was safe at the time the aircraft was released for service.

6. Resolve upper and lower shell fitment by evidence

The listing's YC.JG.ZS002282 is Front Upper Shell; YC.JG.ZS002283 is Front Lower Shell; YC.JG.ZS002432 is Front Lower Shell 2. These names establish three catalog identities, not an interchangeable set. Before ordering, send the dealer the aircraft model and serial identification, photographs of the installed piece and its mating edges, any readable marking on the original component, and the exact material number proposed. Ask for confirmation of revision compatibility and quantity. If the original marking is missing or unreadable, say so; do not fill the gap with a guess based on a similar silhouette or a neighboring product photograph.

During service acceptance, a continuous-looking seam is useful evidence but not sufficient by itself. Request confirmation that all intended retention points are engaged, that the shell has no contact with moving or heat-sensitive components, and that access covers and sensor views remain unobstructed. These are acceptance questions for an authorized technician, not instructions to force-fit a component. Avoid treating a gap tolerance or tightening torque copied from a different Agras model as a T20P specification. If an applicable DJI service document specifies the criterion, use that document and its revision; if none is available to the operator, request the dealer's documented result rather than inventing a number.

7. Do not let small hardware disappear from the work order

The three coded hardware entries require the same discipline as the large shells: YC.WJ.L00870 is named Screw M30-HC060060-55-85; YC.ST.LL000235 is named T30-HC00800080-057030-5103-N; YC.ST.LL000236 is named T20-PC00400040-040010-5103-N. The store calls the latter two mounting hardware, but their names alone do not reveal thread pitch, torque, material grade, interchangeability, or exact quantity per aircraft. An apparently matching generic fastener is therefore not a verified substitute. Give the dealer the material numbers and ask it to establish which original pieces are missing, damaged, reusable, or subject to replacement under its procedure.

YC.JG.ZS002422, Outer Shell Buckle, and YC.JG.ZS000998, Rear Shell Small Buckle Base, are retention-related entries. A broken latch tongue, a distorted base, and a loosened shell screw can produce similar complaints of panel movement; they do not call for the same part. FL.SC.BB000001, Acetate Fabric, is a separately listed service material, not a universal adhesive patch for a cracked cover. The store does not publish a complete installation stack-up or application procedure for these items. Ask qualified service personnel to determine the correct location, quantity and method under current DJI documentation. Record the final installed material numbers rather than only the generic words 'clips and screws.'

8. Keep cleaning and environmental claims inside their boundaries

DJI markets the Agras T20P with IPX6K protection, but its own product explanation warns that the rating is not permanent and may decrease with age and wear. That system-level statement is not a certification for an individual replacement shell, for a cracked seam, or for a port left open after service. The DJI manual also describes a waterproof cover for the front-area USB-C interface and warns that leaving it off after use can admit water and cause a short circuit. A missing cover or visible contamination near an interface deserves its own inspection finding even if the shell itself is intact. Do not infer that a wash test is appropriate merely because the aircraft has a published ingress-protection claim.

Chemical residues and soil also complicate defect assessment: residue can hide a fine crack, while a bright scratch may be mistaken for a fracture. Preserve evidence first, then follow the current DJI cleaning guidance and applicable chemical-handling requirements. Keep the intelligent flight battery and charging equipment outside any aircraft-washing assumption; DJI distinguishes the aircraft from these items in its product guidance. If liquid exposure is suspected at an electrical interface, do not energize the aircraft merely to see whether it still works. The authorized service provider should decide what must be inspected and dried before any controlled power-on.

9. Write a procurement request that can be checked

A useful request has more than a product link. Include the exact T20P model, aircraft serial identifier communicated privately to the dealer, country of operation, event date, affected-side photographs, original component markings, the candidate material numbers from the store listing, required quantities if established, and whether any mating part is also suspected. Ask for confirmation of authenticity, revision applicability, supply status, and the authorized service route. Keep the aircraft identifier and owner contact information out of a public GitHub issue or comment. The listing is an index of selectable parts, not a bill of materials for every damaged aircraft; quantity and applicability require a real inspection.

There is a critical authorization distinction. DJI's T20P manual says damaged-aircraft repair should be referred to DJI Support or a DJI authorized dealer, and that replacement parts should be purchased from an authorized dealer. The existence of a UNITED UAV listing does not establish that UNITED UAV has that authorization, nor does this article make such a claim. Before purchasing for a repair governed by DJI's instruction, confirm the seller's relevant status and the service channel directly. If that cannot be verified, obtain the part through the route DJI specifies. Current availability, specifications, variant nomenclature, warranty terms and regional supply can change, so the live listing and dealer confirmation must govern the final order.

10. Give the service provider a bounded acceptance brief

An acceptance brief can state outcomes without dictating the technician's method: identify the root cause of each logged symptom; inspect adjacent retained parts and any affected cable path; document the installed material numbers and revision; confirm closure of the exterior fit and access-cover concerns; and report the applicable electrical, sensing and spray-system checks performed under DJI procedures. This is especially valuable when an impact produced both a shell defect and an application error. A service ticket saying only 'front cover replaced' does not show whether F02 seam gap, F03 cable abrasion, or a reported camera-view obstruction was resolved. Tie every observed finding to a specific disposition and evidence item.

Do not set an arbitrary field test as the first acceptance gate. The authorized technician should decide the safe order of inspection, reassembly, controlled power-on, system checks and any operational test. Ask for the status of fault messages and a record of the test conditions; 'no error shown' is not equivalent to a structural or environmental integrity check. If a feature cannot be tested at the service location, record it as open rather than marking it passed. The operator should resume missions only after the appropriate provider releases the aircraft, normal preflight checks are completed, and any operational restrictions are understood.

11. Use a traceable before-and-after record

Keep the initial photographs, aircraft identification, selected variant title, material number, purchase or service order, technician's finding, and post-service photographs together in a single maintenance record. Use dates and time zones, not a bare label such as 'last Tuesday.' Preserve a copy of the applicable manual revision and a link to the live product listing, because a store page can change after the work. If the dealer substitutes a superseding part, request written identification of both old and new numbers and the authority for the substitution. This is a configuration-control question, not an attempt to reverse-engineer a DJI part-numbering scheme from the catalog text.

A concise closure matrix can have one line per issue: observation ID, evidence image, suspected component, technician finding, installed part number if any, functional check performed, result, and remaining limitation. For F01, the finding may confirm an upper-shell fracture and a new YC.JG.ZS002282; for F02, it might instead show that a retaining feature was at fault. These examples illustrate record structure, not a diagnosis of a particular aircraft. A part returned unused is also a meaningful result; record it rather than forcing the service story to match the parts ordered in advance. Traceability makes a later recurrence easier to investigate and prevents 'new cover' from becoming a substitute for documented closure.

12. Bound the conclusion and consult the current documents

This guide is deliberately a service-communication and evidence framework. It does not provide disassembly order, tightening torque, wiring pinout, chemical-cleaning recipe, airworthiness release, or a substitute for DJI instructions. Its part identities come from the UNITED UAV T20P front-shell product page. The system context, cleaning limitations and repair boundary come from DJI's official Agras T20P materials. A storefront product title does not prove that a particular replacement has been installed correctly, that a regional dealer is authorized, or that the rest of an impacted aircraft is sound. Those conclusions require the responsible technician's inspection and current manufacturer documentation.

The practical outcome is a more precise handoff: one aircraft, one event narrative, a numbered damage map, the right candidate material numbers, a verified supply route, and an acceptance record that closes each finding. For the current list of selectable variants and availability, consult the linked UNITED UAV product page. For safety requirements and the latest manual, use DJI's official T20P download center and the manufacturer references below. If their instructions conflict with an example in this article, the manufacturer's current instructions control.
