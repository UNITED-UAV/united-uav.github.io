DJI Agras T25 Impeller Pump Parts: A Diagnostic and Service Acceptance Framework

Published 2026-09-20 by UNITED UAV Official

A delivery-pump complaint on a DJI Agras T25 should not begin with a guess about which visible part to replace. The pump sits inside a controlled liquid-delivery chain that includes the tank, strainer, hoses, seals, connectors, electrical command path, flow measurement, paired pumps and atomizing sprinklers. A restriction upstream can resemble a weak pump; air entering at a sealing point can create unstable delivery; a signal fault can leave sound hardware inactive; and a worn hydraulic component can pass a visual inspection yet fail a measured flow check. This guide converts the 17 material-numbered variants in the UNITED UAV T25 impeller-pump listing into a disciplined diagnostic and procurement framework. It is designed for operators, fleet managers and qualified technicians who need traceable evidence, not a speculative parts swap. Current DJI instructions and the responsible service provider's procedures control all disassembly and repair work.

1. Fix the system boundary before diagnosing the pump

DJI specifies two magnetic-drive impeller delivery pumps for the T25. Its current support specifications list a single-pump flow range of 0–12 L/min, a maximum system flow of 16 L/min with two sprinklers, and 24 L/min with four sprinklers. Those numbers describe different boundaries. The 12 L/min figure is a single-pump range; the other two are configuration-level maxima. Adding two single-pump maxima and declaring that every two-sprinkler aircraft must deliver 24 L/min would therefore contradict the stated two-sprinkler system limit. Acceptance criteria must name the aircraft configuration, command setting, test fluid, installed sprinklers and measurement point before a flow number has meaning.

The pump is only one element between liquid in the tank and droplets leaving the aircraft. A sound investigation includes the tank outlet, strainer condition, hose routing, sealing points, pump inlet and outlet, electrical connection, flow-control command, flow measurement, and sprinkler condition. The objective is not to prove that the pump is guilty; it is to isolate where commanded delivery stops matching measured delivery. Start with the least invasive evidence and keep the aircraft grounded until the leak, electrical fault, abnormal noise or unexplained delivery deviation has been resolved under the applicable DJI procedure.

2. Record the complaint as data, not an adjective

Terms such as weak, intermittent, noisy and leaking are useful alerts but poor acceptance criteria. Convert the report into observable fields: date and time; aircraft identifier; flight and pump hours if available; two- or four-sprinkler configuration; product previously handled; commanded application rate; displayed and independently collected flow; left-right behavior; tank level; ambient temperature; warning text; visible leakage location; and whether the symptom began suddenly or developed over several missions. Preserve screenshots and controller logs where the approved workflow allows. Do not run a pesticide merely to reproduce a fault that can be investigated with the manufacturer's permitted clean-water maintenance procedure.

Separate the symptom from its consequence. Uneven application can result from unequal pump delivery, but also from a blocked sprinkler, a hose restriction, air ingestion, an incorrect configuration, or operating parameters. A reported empty-tank mismatch can involve delivery or measurement. A wet pump housing may indicate an upstream connection, a pump-interface seal, or liquid migrating from elsewhere. Use one evidence identifier per observation—for example, P01 for measured left-channel output and L01 for a photographed leak trace—so that the service report can close every original finding without rewriting history after a part is replaced.

3. Use the 17 material numbers as a controlled vocabulary

The store listing identifies the following pump-assembly items: YC.WJ.L00870, Screw M30-HC060060-55-85; YC.JG.ZS002389, Impeller Pump Protective Hood; BC.AG.SS000543, Impeller Pump Motor; YC.JG.MY000704, Motor Sealing Ring; YC.JG.MY000712, Centrifugal Valve Rubber Pad; YC.JG.ZS003418, Impeller Pump Shell; YC.JG.ZS002233, Water Outlet Rotation Cover; YC.JG.MY001053, Y-tee Part Sealing Pad; and YC.XC.XX001002, Spraying Signal Cable. These names distinguish fastening, protection, drive, sealing, hydraulic enclosure and signal functions, but they do not by themselves establish failure, required quantity or compatibility with every production revision.

The remaining entries are YC.JG.ZS003420, Impeller Pump Curving Connector; YC.XJ.QT000350, Pump Connector Sealing Ring; YC.JG.QX002485, Anti-Wear Gasket; YC.JG.MY000832, Damping Pad; YC.WJ.LL000138, Screw M25-HC00400040-050020-0003-N; YC.JG.ZS003417, Impeller Pump Spacing Sleeve; BC.AG.SS000710, Impeller Pump Module; and YC.JG.MY001052, Pump Sealing Ring. Keep the displayed material number separate from the store SKU, because some SKUs carry suffixes. A procurement record should contain the diagram item number, exact material number, material name, selected variant, quantity and the technician's reason for replacement.

4. Establish a safe hydraulic baseline

A baseline test must be repeatable. Under the current DJI instructions and site safety controls, use the approved clean-water procedure, a known starting volume or calibrated collection method, the same sprinkler configuration on each comparison, and a defined command duration. Record the command level, elapsed time and collected volume for each channel. Calculated average flow is collected volume divided by elapsed time; convert units consistently before comparison. Repeat the measurement rather than trusting one short run, because timing error and retained liquid can dominate a small sample. Stop if the test reveals leakage, dry running, electrical warnings, abnormal heating or a sound that the service procedure treats as unsafe.

For paired-pump comparison, define normalized imbalance as the absolute difference between left and right measured flow divided by their mean, multiplied by 100 percent. This ratio is useful for trending, but this article does not invent a pass limit. Use a limit in current DJI service documentation or one approved by the responsible technician. If both channels are similarly low, investigate common upstream restrictions, configuration and test setup before condemning two pumps at once. If only one channel is low, swap only those non-invasive comparison conditions that the official procedure permits and preserve the original configuration in the record.

5. Isolate zero-flow and no-start complaints

A zero-flow complaint has at least four branches: no valid command reaches the channel; the electrical path is interrupted; the pump is commanded but mechanically unable to deliver; or liquid cannot reach or leave the pump. Begin by confirming the exact controller warning and whether one or both channels are affected. Inspect only accessible connectors and harness routing allowed by the maintenance procedure. The store's YC.XC.XX001002 Spraying Signal Cable is a candidate component, not proof that every no-start event is a cable failure. Likewise, BC.AG.SS000543 identifies the motor, while BC.AG.SS000710 identifies the impeller pump module; ordering both without isolation evidence defeats the purpose of modular service.

Hydraulic checks should move from common to local causes. Confirm approved test liquid is present, the strainer is not blocked, hoses are not visibly pinched, and required connections are seated and leak-free. DJI's safety guidance explicitly calls for filtered mixed liquid and clearing blockages before operation. If the controller reports a command yet one pump remains inactive, the technician can use the manufacturer procedure to distinguish the signal cable, connector, motor and pump module. Do not bypass interlocks, apply external power to an unidentified motor, or energize a wet connector as a shortcut. Those actions can destroy evidence and create a new electrical hazard.

6. Diagnose low or unstable flow without blaming the motor first

Low flow can originate before, inside or after the pump. Upstream candidates include a partially blocked strainer, restricted hose, insufficient liquid head or air entering a connector seal. Pump-level candidates include impeller wear, interference, internal leakage or a drive problem. Downstream candidates include a restricted hose, sprinkler contamination or a configuration mismatch. Unstable output is especially compatible with intermittent air ingestion, variable restriction or command instability, although none should be declared from symptoms alone. Compare left and right channels under identical conditions and document whether the displayed flow and physically collected flow move together.

Use deviation in two ways. Command-tracking deviation equals measured minus commanded flow, while repeatability can be summarized by the range or standard deviation of several equal-duration runs. A stable but biased result suggests a different path from a result that surges between runs. If the controller's value looks steady while collection varies, measurement location and timing need review; if both vary together, the delivery chain may actually be unstable. Do not apply a generic percentage threshold copied from another aircraft. The pass band must come from current T25 documentation, a validated fleet baseline or the accountable service provider.

7. Treat leakage and air ingress as different questions

External liquid leakage and inward air leakage can share a damaged sealing interface, but they are not operationally identical. Liquid traces can often be photographed; air ingress may reveal itself through unstable flow, bubbles or failure to maintain delivery without producing a large visible wet area. The T25 listing includes several distinct sealing-related items: YC.JG.MY000704 Motor Sealing Ring, YC.JG.MY000712 Centrifugal Valve Rubber Pad, YC.JG.MY001053 Y-tee Part Sealing Pad, YC.XJ.QT000350 Pump Connector Sealing Ring and YC.JG.MY001052 Pump Sealing Ring. Similar-sounding names are not interchangeable seals.

Map the first observed wet point before wiping the system, but account for gravity, airflow and liquid migration. Photograph the interface, adjacent connector and the route above it. A seal should not be ordered merely because it is the nearest item in the diagram; the mating surfaces, connector, shell and retention hardware also require evaluation. After service, acceptance should check the repaired interface under the manufacturer-approved clean-water condition and then inspect it after pressure or flow has returned to zero. A dry exterior during a short static check does not by itself demonstrate stable flow or absence of air ingress.

8. Use sound, vibration and heat as corroborating evidence

A new tonal sound, rattling, cyclic vibration or rising temperature can help localize a problem, but none identifies a part number on its own. The protective hood, shell, anti-wear gasket, damping pad, spacing sleeve and fastening hardware all influence mechanical support or protection around the assembly. A loose cover can create noise without reducing hydraulic output; an internal impeller issue can reduce output without obvious housing damage. Record when the sound begins, whether it changes with command level, and whether it occurs on one or both channels. Do not place hands near powered or moving equipment to feel vibration.

A useful comparison is a short, controlled recording of both channels under identical approved conditions, paired with flow data and controller status. Mark microphone distance and avoid drawing conclusions from absolute loudness recorded by different phones. Temperature evidence should come from a method approved for the service environment and should compare equivalent locations after equal duty, not arbitrary hot spots. If an electrical smell, rapid heating, melted material or seized behavior is present, stop the test. The purpose of evidence is to narrow the technician's inspection, not to operate a failing part until its cause becomes visually dramatic.

9. Separate the hydraulic module from the signal chain

The listing distinguishes a Spraying Signal Cable, an Impeller Pump Motor and an Impeller Pump Module. Preserve that separation in the fault tree. A command-path problem may prevent a healthy motor from running. A motor can respond electrically while the hydraulic module fails to produce the expected flow. A mechanically sound pump can appear weak if a connector seal admits air or a downstream restriction changes the operating point. The technician should correlate controller commands, logged faults, electrical checks permitted by DJI, observed operation and collected flow before assigning the root cause.

Do not use parts substitution as the first electrical test when a controlled diagnostic is available. Swapping channels or modules can move contamination, disturb seals, introduce connector damage, and make the original fault disappear without explanation. If the approved DJI procedure calls for a swap or measured electrical check, record the before-and-after configuration and restore traceability. Any repaired cable path should be accepted for secure seating, clearance, strain relief and absence of liquid contamination. A successful power-on is necessary evidence for some repairs, but it is not proof of hydraulic accuracy, sealing integrity or correct long-term cable routing.

10. Decide between component replacement and module replacement

A component-level repair is justified only when the failed item and all affected interfaces are identified, the procedure permits that level of service, and the result can be tested. A module replacement may reduce assembly uncertainty but should not be used to hide an upstream cause that would damage the new part. For example, replacing BC.AG.SS000710 without clearing contamination, correcting a pinched line or confirming the seal stack can reproduce the complaint. Conversely, replacing only an inexpensive ring when the mating connector or pump shell is damaged can produce a temporary dry result that fails under operation.

Use a decision record with five fields: evidence supporting the failed item; collateral interfaces inspected; applicable DJI service instruction; selected material number and revision; and the post-repair verification that closes the original symptom. The store lists YC.JG.ZS003418 as the Impeller Pump Shell, YC.JG.ZS003420 as the Curving Connector, and YC.JG.ZS002233 as the Water Outlet Rotation Cover. Their names do not publish wear limits, torque values or interchange rules. Request those criteria from current DJI documentation or a qualified service provider instead of inferring them from photographs or part geometry.

11. Control fasteners, spacing and protection as part of the assembly

The catalog includes two separately identified screws: YC.WJ.L00870, Screw M30-HC060060-55-85, and YC.WJ.LL000138, Screw M25-HC00400040-050020-0003-N. It also lists YC.JG.ZS003417, Impeller Pump Spacing Sleeve, YC.JG.QX002485, Anti-Wear Gasket, YC.JG.MY000832, Damping Pad, and YC.JG.ZS002389, Impeller Pump Protective Hood. Do not replace these with generic hardware based on visual similarity. The material number is the procurement identity; installation location, quantity, reuse policy and tightening method belong to the current service data.

After authorized service, inspect the observable outcome rather than improvising a torque audit. The assembly should be aligned, retained and clear of adjacent hoses and cables; the protective hood should not contact moving or hot parts; and there should be no new abrasion path. Ask the technician to document which fasteners were renewed and which were reused under the procedure. A missing small gasket or sleeve can affect alignment or wear even when the pump initially meets flow. Configuration control therefore includes every installed interface item, not only the expensive motor or module.

12. Build a clean-water acceptance test with explicit gates

Acceptance should proceed in gates. Gate 1 is a powered-off inspection for correct part identity, connector seating, hose routing, retained hardware and a dry exterior. Gate 2 is an approved clean-water static check for visible leakage. Gate 3 is a controlled pump run that records command, collected volume, time, controller indications and left-right behavior. Gate 4 verifies the downstream sprinklers and checks that the original application symptom is no longer present under a representative but safe condition. Gate 5 is documentation closure. If any gate fails, stop and return to diagnosis rather than compensating with a higher command setting.

For every timed collection, record uncertainty. A container marked only in large increments cannot prove a small improvement, and manual start-stop timing adds error. Use the same equipment and duration for paired comparisons. Compare the result to the applicable DJI criterion and the configured system limit; do not demand the headline maximum under conditions for which DJI does not specify it. The official maximum is a capability boundary, not a universal acceptance target at every command, fluid, sprinkler configuration and test setup. A technically valid report can conclude that the system meets the approved test criterion without claiming that every possible field application has been validated.

13. Connect maintenance intervals to real operating records

DJI's T50/T25 manual identifies the delivery pump for inspection every 100 operating hours or one month and recommends pump replacement after 500 hours and motor replacement after 1,000 hours. Consult the current manual for the exact applicable wording and any regional or revision update. Calendar time and operating hours answer different degradation questions, so retain both. A fleet that reaches 100 hours in two weeks should not wait for the month boundary; a low-use aircraft should not ignore calendar inspection. Record pump and motor identity after replacement so the component's own service age is not confused with total aircraft age.

Daily process quality matters as much as interval maintenance. DJI instructs operators to use clean water when mixing, filter mixed liquid before filling, clear blockages before use, clean the spraying system after operation, and clean sprinklers promptly. Its quick-start maintenance sequence describes circulating clean or soapy water through the sprinklers and cleaning removable strainers and sprinklers, subject to the current instructions. The exact chemical, rinse and waste-disposal procedure must also comply with the product label and local environmental rules. Never discharge pesticide or contaminated rinse water merely to complete a pump test.

14. Procure by verified fitment, then close the record

A complete purchase request should include the aircraft model, privately communicated serial identifier, installed spraying configuration, diagram item number, exact material number, material name, quantity, photographs of the original part and interfaces, and the technician's fitment confirmation. Ask whether a material number has been superseded and record both old and new numbers if so. Current stock status on the live store page is not proof of compatibility with a specific serial range. The 17 variants are separately selectable components, not a guaranteed kit, and a list position does not define required quantity.

Close the service with a compact evidence table: complaint ID, baseline measurement, root-cause finding, replaced material number, collateral parts inspected, clean-water result, remaining limitations, responsible technician and date. Preserve the applicable DJI manual revision and the public product URL. If the dealer cannot confirm fitment or the repair falls outside the operator's authorized maintenance scope, stop and escalate to DJI Support or a qualified service channel. The professional outcome is not simply a pump that runs; it is a traceable spraying system whose original fault has been isolated, corrected and verified against a defined criterion.
